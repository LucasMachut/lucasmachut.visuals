What I look for

The moment a person and a place become one

A wedding, a retreat, a gathering. Never just the place, never just the person.

Work

I don't sort the work by occasion

What changes between a wedding and a retreat is the reason people gathered. The light I look for, the distance I keep and the moment I press the shutter stay the same.

Four series, held together by what repeats from one image to the next.

I the body the flower, the shade, the palms, the water
II the hands the water, the sand, the sheet
III the faces what two people do to one another
IV the light from midday to the lampshade
How I work

I don't direct from a distance: I step into the scene

I step into the scene, let people settle, and wait. What remains actually happened.

Where it comes from

An outside eye, inside the scene

I'm French and I live in Brazil. I see from outside what the people inside have stopped noticing. Before images, my trade was interpreting: years spent watching gestures and what people say without speaking.

The eye

Each of us builds their own world.

Maybe that's what fascinates me most about photography. An image holds an instant, but we never really know what that instant meant to the person in it. How important, or how ordinary, it was for them.

We each start from a different view of the world. With a photograph you can try to build a bridge, but everyone will see what they want to see and what they are able to see.

A humility I like to keep close.

Hotels · Guesthouses · Retreats

Some places are felt even with no one there

In the rest of my work, the person is what makes the place exist. In a hotel the challenge is the opposite: the place has to be felt on its own, before any guest arrives.

For hotels, guesthouses and retreats, there is separate work.
